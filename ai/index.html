Product architecture

GC Academy: an adaptive K–5 learning platform

In production

A subscription learning platform for children, with a parent dashboard, individualized learning paths and weekly progress reports. I set the architecture and product rules, and directed an AI-assisted build against them.

Problem

  • Parents need visibility into what a child actually learned, without extra work.
  • Paid content must stay behind a membership gate while a free sample stays public.
  • Children’s data demands consent and privacy by design.

Design decisions

  • Static front end with serverless functions, so there are no servers to run.
  • An edge function sends members-only files only to signed-in members.
  • Question banks pass a validator that enforces written quality rules.
  • Parent consent and card verification before any child information is shared.

AI-assisted multi-state standards crosswalk

In practice

A secondary Economics and Financial Literacy program was failing state compliance and needed to expand to five more states. I used AI analysis to map the courseware against each state’s graduation standards, surface gaps, and drive targeted lesson redesign.

Outcome

  • Standards crosswalks for six states.
  • Gap analysis, new gap-filling lessons and a master curriculum map.
  • State approval with 100% compliance on the program.

Design decisions

  • AI proposes mappings; a subject-matter reviewer approves every one.
  • Gaps are measured against depth-of-knowledge levels, not keywords alone.
  • Every output lands in a document the state reviewer can verify.

Style Studio: a real-time virtual styling engine

In production

A free, no-sign-up browser game where children style characters with outfits, hair color and makeup, then save or print their looks. Under the hood it is a compositing engine: it recolors artwork, maps printed fabrics onto garments, cleans cut-out edges, and fits every item to every character. I specified the product rules and quality bar, and directed an AI-assisted build against them.

Problem

  • Hundreds of item and character combinations cannot each be drawn by hand.
  • Layered clothing showed skin patches, ragged edges and misaligned makeup.
  • Children’s play needs privacy by default: no accounts, chat or sharing.

Design decisions

  • One data-driven catalog; new items are data, not new code.
  • Recoloring and fabric mapping reuse one artwork shape for many colors and prints.
  • Edge cleanup pass: despeckle, contour smoothing, fringe removal.
  • Lip and makeup masks derived per character from color analysis, not hand-drawn.
  • Looks stay on the device; unlocks use codes behind a grown-up gate.

Standards-grounded RAG tutor, with an evaluation set

Building

Ask a question about Texas K–5 math and get the matching standard with its 19 TAC citation, or an honest “I can’t find that.” The first version uses keyword retrieval (BM25) over all 288 official standards and quotes the standard rather than generating text, so every answer is grounded by construction. The evaluation set matters as much as the app: it measures whether the right standard is found and whether the system declines when it should.

Try it — live

Source: 19 TAC Chapter 111, Subchapter A (Texas Education Agency, published for information only). Keyword retrieval, no AI-generated text, nothing you type is stored.

Held-out results (20 answerable and 6 off-topic questions I wrote): the right standard was the top result for 16 of 20 and in the top three for all 20; all 6 off-topic questions were declined; 1 of 20 real questions was wrongly declined. Small sample, one author, not yet teacher-reviewed.
